# UNI-COL-001 — Colony Survival, Local Suitability and Industrial Dependency

**Owner:** MineIT-Universe, shared design · **Status:** lore-grounded cross-game principles; platform balance and simulation rates not universal · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-14.
**Sources:** [Crash-world lore, §§20–21](../../../data/lore/Koplin_Universe_Expanded_Backstory_Lore_Bible.md), [Deep Reach charter, §19](../../../data/lore/Koplin_Scenario_II_Deep_Reach_Mining_Charter.md), [Shared industrial model](../industrial-economy/01-industrial-model.md).

## Shared survival model

A settlement survives only when **actually viable Food, Water, Air, Shelter, appropriate Power/Heat, health/safety and labour** meet its environment and population. A beautiful-looking world is not necessarily nutritionally or chemically safe. A mechanically equipped farm still cannot grow unsupported crops in unsuitable conditions without controlled life support. An inhabited station requires supported breathing, temperature and water loops; an airless moon needs sealed habitats and energy.

**Physical observations, analyses and established use are distinct**:
- a body can visibly contain a forest, water or herds before anyone knows whether they are chemically safe;
- surveying/collection yields persistent source identity, biological analysis or physical composition, not automatically an edible item;
- local Food expansion may require seed, breeding stock, feed, water and suitable climate;
- some non-food native life can remain useful for fuel or industrial chemistry.

## Different canonical starting situations

The **Crash/Far Lantern scenario (Desktop)** begins with compromised ship reactor cooling and finite supplies. The lore's 'approximately one year' reactor horizon is a maintenance estimate under conservative load; the 'six-month' food/medical supply horizon is a narrative opening constraint, not a hard automatic timer shared across products.

The **Year-5326 Deep Reach commercial scenario (Mobile)** starts with an industrial settlement package, controlled Food support and contractual supply guarantees. Its challenge is replacing imported consumables and machinery support economically, not replicating an isolated crashed expedition's exact initial manifest.

Neither game may copy the other's starting population, generator inventory, rationing numbers or calendar deadlines as universal world facts.

## Recovery and priorities

A genuine shortage or failed life support has consequences. Both products should allow coherent engineering recovery where salvageable stock, workforce, Power or accessible replenishment remain possible. Prioritising essentials ahead of surplus commercial output is a general design direction; **precise priority ordering and rationing curves belong to each platform**.

The same capability may be provided by different facilities: a power station, distributed renewable systems, ship fallback or station reactor, all subject to actual local suitability and maintenance. Company-wide inventory is not magically available at each remote site.

## Service dependence rather than arbitrary Industry

A mine can be structurally present, correctly staffed, power-fed and equipment-fit, while still needing replacement Parts. The shared physical design explains this through maintainable machines, **not** arbitrary generic Ore -> universal Industry points. A compromised Power plant may also require spares unavailable at a given outpost. The specific derating/degradation response remains [Mobile D01–D03](https://github.com/kevvy555/MineIT-Android/issues/114) and an independent Desktop design choice.

## Platform ownership and future opportunities

- **Desktop:** individual settlers, food viability through analysis, water contamination, physical Power dependencies, reactor salvage and generational employment.
- **Mobile:** aggregated workforce/colonies, eco-stock/animal feeds, consumable inventory, emergency-mode priority and years of simulation.
- **Shared:** no magically interchangeable survival consumable; real location, compatible technology, physical environment and working equipment always matter.

**Open:** common semantic conditions for 'breathable/safe water/edible', cross-platform biological identity, and shared hazard taxonomy. Do not invent an authored alien substance or health law in this chapter.
