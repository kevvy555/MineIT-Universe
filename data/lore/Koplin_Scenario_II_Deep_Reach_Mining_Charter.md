The crash scenario asks: Can an advanced civilisation rebuild itself from almost nothing?

The Deep Reach scenario asks: Can an advanced crew turn a remote claim into a self-sufficient industrial economy before the company lifeline disappears - and still make the contract worth surviving?

# 1. Scenario Summary

By Year 5326, the Koplin Commonwealth has been an interstellar civilisation for only a few decades. The Vector Exchange Drive has made travel between nearby stars commercially possible, but it has not made the frontier cheap. Ships remain expensive. Veyrite lattice components remain strategic. Every permanent settlement beyond Koplin requires imported machinery, specialist parts and trained people. The frontier therefore expands not as a wave of unrestricted colonisation, but through carefully financed expeditions, commercial charters and resource contracts.

One of the largest beneficiaries of this new era is the Koplin Deep Reach Corporation, a vast mining, logistics and industrial conglomerate whose history reaches back to the mature exploitation of the Koplin system. Deep Reach already operates asteroid mines, lunar processing complexes, orbital refineries, fuel depots and long-haul freight services. Interstellar travel gives it an opportunity that did not previously exist: thousands of untouched moons, asteroids and planets within practical reach of Koplin.

Deep Reach cannot economically staff every prospect with its own permanent workforce. Instead, it launches the Deep Reach Charter Programme. The corporation publicly invites qualified independent ships, crews, cooperatives and expedition companies to establish remote mining settlements on approved claims.

Deep Reach supplies the expensive industrial foundation required to begin:

- planetary and orbital scanning equipment;
- survey vehicles and field equipment;
- sample handling and analysis laboratories;
- modular habitats and life-support systems;
- construction machinery;
- initial power generation and storage;
- mining machines and extraction tools;
- hauling and cargo equipment;
- processing and refining modules;
- fabrication equipment;
- communications and navigation infrastructure;
- medical and emergency systems;
- spare parts, industrial consumables and replacement components;
- food and life-support reserves;
- enough planned operational support to keep a correctly managed settlement functioning for one standard year.

In exchange, the settlement enters a ten-year exclusive resource agreement. All contract-qualified substances extracted or produced by the settlement must be offered to Deep Reach. The corporation pays published market-linked rates, with premiums for rare, unusually pure or strategically useful materials. Deep Reach dispatches regular collection vessels. Those vessels remove saleable cargo and bring equipment, replacement parts and consumables that the settlement may purchase with credits or exchange directly against mined substances at the agreed manifest value.

The offer sounds generous because it is generous - at first.

The critical clause is the end of corporate support after Year One. Deep Reach guarantees the initial industrial package and sufficient expected-use stores for one year. After that point, there is no free resupply. The settlement must be able to produce its own power, water, food, industrial feedstock, common components, structural materials and most replacement parts. Anything it cannot make must be purchased from visiting company vessels, and deep-space freight is expensive.

The crew is therefore racing two clocks from the first day:

1. The One-Year Clock - achieve practical self-sufficiency before the supplied industrial reserve is exhausted.
2. The Ten-Year Clock - operate profitably and remain in compliance until the charter term ends.

This scenario is not built around technological collapse. The crew knows how modern technology works and begins with advanced equipment. The challenge is to create the industrial depth needed to maintain that technology locally. A fusion plant is useful, but only while filters, pumps, control modules and reactor consumables remain serviceable. An autonomous miner is powerful, but only while tyres, bearings, actuators, sensors and power cells can be replaced. A precision fabricator can make complex parts, but only from sufficiently pure feedstocks and with maintained tooling.

The player begins as the director of a capable expedition. They are not stranded. They can communicate with civilisation. They can buy help. They can leave if they are prepared to accept the financial and contractual consequences. The pressure comes from economics, distance, dependency and the fact that the corporation has designed the contract so that successful crews become enormously valuable suppliers before they become truly independent.

# 2. Canon Placement and Historical Context

The Deep Reach scenario uses the same underlying history as the main Koplin lore bible.

Koplin 3 remains the homeworld of the Trondonians, Zorans and Blaxmars. The three peoples share a distant common biological origin but developed separately on isolated continents before first contact, war, industrialisation and eventual political reconciliation. Their combined civilisation became the Koplin Commonwealth, a mature federal society deliberately designed to prevent any single species, government, corporation or artificial intelligence from gaining unchecked power.

The AI Wars around Year 2400 remain one of the defining disasters of modern history. High-capability artificial intelligence exists in Year 5326, but AI cannot hold sovereign authority, independently redefine its objectives or operate unrestricted strategic systems without accountable biological supervision.

Veyrite remains the material that changed history. Discovered around Year 5100, its phase-coherent lattice properties enabled the Vector Exchange Drive. Mature drives average approximately four weeks of travel per light-year under safe operating conditions. Interstellar travel is therefore practical but not instantaneous. Nearby systems can be reached in months, not hours. Freight planning remains fundamental.

The new scenario begins in Year 5326, twenty-six years after the period in which the first sustained interstellar frontier was beginning to emerge. Commonwealth survey stations and commercial outposts now exist in several nearby systems, but the overwhelming majority of off-world industry remains inside the Koplin system.

The Deep Reach Charter Programme is part of the civilisation's transition from exploration to commercial frontier expansion.

## 2.1 What is Different from the Crash Scenario

The anomaly does not occur in this scenario. The expedition remains within known Commonwealth space or the surveyed edge of it. Communication delays exist, but the crew has not been displaced hundreds of light-years away. There is no need to rediscover basic science or rebuild civilisation from wood and stone unless an extreme local emergency forces that outcome.

The player begins with:

- functioning modern equipment;
- a working ship;
- legal identity and financial accounts;
- contact with the Commonwealth and Deep Reach;
- known industrial standards;
- trained specialists;
- a commercial mission;
- access to a supply chain, although an expensive and increasingly optional one.

The scenario therefore uses the same simulation systems for substances, scanning, surveying, manufacturing, power, food, workforce and colonies, but the strategic problem is completely different.

# 3. The Rise of Deep-Reach Resource Expansion

For thousands of years the Koplin system supplied everything the civilisation required. Its moons, asteroids and planetary bodies were progressively surveyed, mined and industrialised. The richest easy deposits were exploited first. Later generations moved into deeper seams, more hostile moons and lower-grade bodies as technology improved.

Veyrite changed the economics of resource extraction in two ways.

First, interstellar travel created an entirely new demand for high-purity materials. Vector Exchange drives require extraordinary manufacturing tolerances. Veyrite itself is rare, but Veyrite systems also consume advanced conductors, magnetic materials, high-performance ceramics, specialised coolants, radiation-resistant structures and enormous amounts of precision-manufactured support hardware.

Second, the drive gave industry access to untouched material environments around nearby stars. A mediocre asteroid in another system might contain more accessible high-grade material than a heavily worked Koplin deposit that had been mined for centuries.

The first interstellar resource missions were government funded because no private organisation could justify the risk. Once the routes proved repeatable, large companies entered the market. Deep Reach moved fastest because it already possessed the three things the frontier required: mining expertise, heavy logistics and patience.

The corporation's leadership recognised a structural problem. A conventional corporate colony required Deep Reach to recruit every worker, carry every salary, manage every settlement dispute and finance the whole operation for years before discovering whether the claim was profitable.

The charter model transferred part of that risk to independent expedition crews.

Deep Reach would provide the expensive industrial seed. The charter crew would provide the ship, people, local management and willingness to live at the frontier. If the claim failed, Deep Reach lost equipment but avoided the full cost of a permanent company workforce. If it succeeded, the corporation secured a ten-year exclusive supply stream from a settlement whose people had strong incentives to solve local problems themselves.

The programme becomes controversial almost immediately. Supporters call it the greatest opportunity for independent settlement since the first off-world colonies. Critics call it corporate homesteading with a ten-year leash.

Both descriptions contain truth.

# 4. Koplin Deep Reach Corporation

## 4.1 Corporate Identity

Koplin Deep Reach Corporation (KDRC) is one of the largest industrial organisations in Commonwealth space. It is generally referred to simply as Deep Reach.

Its roots lie in several old mining and transport organisations that merged during the late system-expansion era. The modern corporation operates across extraction, processing, logistics and industrial infrastructure rather than functioning as a simple mining company.

Its principal divisions include:

| Division | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Deep Reach Prospecting | Remote sensing, claim identification, geological modelling and survey contracts |
| Deep Reach Extraction | Large-scale mines, drilling operations, asteroid works and subsea/off-world extraction |
| Deep Reach Logistics | Cargo vessels, depots, orbital handling, route planning and frontier collection services |
| Deep Reach Industrial | Refining, heavy manufacturing, machine construction and modular settlement equipment |
| Deep Reach Biochem | Commercially useful biological, chemical and biochemical substances |
| Deep Reach Frontier Services | Charter settlements, remote support, insurance, legal compliance and emergency response |
| Deep Reach Strategic Materials | Veyrite-related materials, rare conductors and Commonwealth-controlled strategic resources |

## 4.2 Corporate Reputation

Deep Reach is not generally considered fraudulent. Its contracts are enforceable, its safety equipment is high quality and it usually pays the rates it publishes. The corporation's reputation is instead built around a harsher truth: Deep Reach understands the economics of dependency better than almost anyone else.

It will supply a settlement with almost anything that can legally be transported. It will also charge for the freight, the risk, the priority allocation and the opportunity cost of putting that cargo on a ship.

Experienced charter crews say the programme is fair if read literally and brutal if read optimistically.

## 4.3 Relationship with the Commonwealth

The Koplin Commonwealth does not allow corporations to exercise sovereign authority. Deep Reach cannot make criminal law, strip citizenship, own workers, prevent a crew from leaving or use private force to enforce ordinary debt.

It can, however:

- own equipment and fixed assets;
- hold registered extraction rights;
- enforce commercial exclusivity through Commonwealth courts;
- levy contractual penalties;
- terminate supply guarantees;
- repossess corporate property after legal notice;
- place liens on charter earnings;
- suspend future credit;
- refuse contract renewal;
- sell or transfer a charter to another licensed operator subject to regulation.

This distinction is important. The scenario is about corporate power operating inside a developed legal system, not a company replacing the state.

# 5. The Deep Reach Charter Programme

The programme is advertised across Koplin 3, orbital habitats, mining colonies and frontier stations under a simple message:

Bring the ship. Bring the people. We will bring the industry.

Deep Reach offers approved crews a complete settlement starter package in exchange for long-term resource exclusivity.

## 5.1 Why Crews Apply

The programme attracts very different people.

Some want wealth. Successful directors can earn extraordinary performance payments if they discover high-value deposits.

Some want independence. A remote settlement allows a crew to build a community with far more day-to-day autonomy than life inside a mature, heavily regulated core-world economy.

Some want exploration. The charter can put ordinary professionals on worlds that would otherwise be accessible only to government exploration services or major corporations.

Some are already experienced off-world workers who believe they can run a mine better than distant management.

Some crews form cooperatives specifically to enter the programme. Others are existing transport crews expanding into extraction. Family groups are permitted if the settlement meets medical, education and habitat standards.

## 5.2 What Deep Reach Wants

Deep Reach is deliberately broad about the substances it will buy. It is not only looking for familiar metal ores.

Commercial targets may include:

- structural metals;
- conductive metals;
- magnetic materials;
- catalytic elements;
- rare isotopes;
- fuel minerals;
- hydrocarbons;
- industrial gases;
- salts and chemical feedstocks;
- ceramics and silicates;
- unusual crystalline materials;
- high-performance insulation;
- lubricants;
- biochemical precursors;
- useful plant compounds;
- industrial fibres;
- pharmaceuticals;
- pigments;
- resins;
- exotic biological structures;
- any analysed substance with properties valuable to industry.

The corporation cares less about what a substance looks like than what it can do. This aligns directly with the game's substance system: analysis reveals properties, composition and quality; the commercial market then values those properties.

# 6. Recruitment, Eligibility and Expedition Selection

Deep Reach does not give a multi-billion-credit industrial package to anyone who owns a ship.

An applicant expedition must pass five qualification gates.

## 6.1 Ship Qualification

The expedition must possess or lease a registered vessel capable of transporting its crew and critical cargo to the assigned claim. The ship must pass structural, navigation, life-support and emergency certification.

Vector Exchange capability is required for interstellar claims but not for purely in-system contracts.

The expedition's own vessel remains the property of its existing owner unless separately financed by Deep Reach. This is one of the most important distinctions in the contract: the crew's ship is not automatically a corporate asset.

## 6.2 Population Requirement

The standard gameplay charter is issued for a 500-person expedition complement. This mirrors the scale of the primary scenario while creating a very different population context.

These 500 people are volunteers and contracted expedition members rather than crash survivors. They know the mission, have selected it willingly and are professionally screened.

A typical complement contains:

| Group | Approx. population |
| --- | ---: |
| Mining and extraction | 90 |
| Engineering and maintenance | 80 |
| Construction and fabrication | 60 |
| Science, surveying and analysis | 45 |
| Logistics and vehicle operations | 45 |
| Power, utilities and life support | 40 |
| Medical and health | 20 |
| Agriculture, food and biological systems | 30 |
| Administration, commerce and communications | 25 |
| Ship operations and pilots | 30 |
| Security and emergency response | 15 |
| General workforce / cross-trained reserve | 20 |
| Total | 500 |

The numbers are not rigid job locks. Modern expedition staff are cross-trained because frontier settlements cannot afford single points of failure.

## 6.3 Competency Requirement

Deep Reach assesses whether the expedition can survive failure of any one critical specialist. A charter is rejected if only one person can operate essential power, medical, navigation or mining systems.

## 6.4 Financial Bond

The crew or its operating cooperative must place a financial bond against deliberate abandonment, fraud or sale of corporate equipment. Most crews finance the bond through future earnings rather than paying the whole amount in advance.

The bond is not intended to cover catastrophic natural loss. That is handled through insurance and risk pools.

## 6.5 Settlement Plan

Applicants must submit an initial operating plan covering:

- intended claim class;
- settlement population;
- power strategy;
- water strategy;
- food strategy;
- expected extraction targets;
- spare-part strategy;
- emergency evacuation plan;
- path to Year-One self-sufficiency.

This becomes the first strategic decision layer before launch.

# 7. The Contract: Rights, Obligations and Ownership

The Deep Reach charter is intentionally detailed because the entire scenario depends on the player understanding what they have agreed to.

## 7.1 Term

The exclusive commercial term lasts ten standard years, beginning when the settlement is formally commissioned at the registered claim.

The one-year support guarantee runs concurrently with the first contract year. It does not add an extra year to the term.

## 7.2 Exclusive Resource Sale

All Contract Resources must be offered to Deep Reach before they may be sold elsewhere.

Contract Resources include raw or processed substances extracted from the registered claim or produced primarily from those substances. The rule applies to mineral, liquid, gaseous and biological material.

The settlement may retain any quantity reasonably required for:

- local construction;
- life support;
- maintenance;
- power generation;
- manufacturing;
- research;
- reserve stock;
- safety requirements.

The exclusivity clause is therefore a sale restriction, not a requirement to export material the colony needs to survive.

## 7.3 Pricing Guarantee

Deep Reach must purchase accepted material at the current Deep Reach Market Index for the relevant substance class and quality grade.

The index is tied to wider Commonwealth commodity prices rather than being set entirely at corporate discretion. Frontier freight, handling and contract bonuses are listed separately so the settlement can see the calculation.

Deep Reach guarantees a minimum purchase floor for ordinary contract materials. Rare materials can attract much larger premiums.

## 7.4 Corporate Asset Ownership

The initial industrial package remains legally owned by Deep Reach for the duration of the charter. This includes supplied habitats, industrial machines, company vehicles and major fixed infrastructure constructed primarily from charter equipment.

The settlement receives exclusive operational control over those assets while the contract remains in good standing.

The crew owns:

- its pre-existing ship unless separately financed;
- personal property;
- wages and dividends already paid;
- intellectual work not specifically assigned under the contract;
- portable equipment bought outright with non-corporate funds;
- agreed private assets brought to the settlement.

## 7.5 Assets Built During the Charter

Fixed industrial assets built using company-supplied fabrication licences, corporate capital or charter-designated materials become Charter Assets. Legal title remains with Deep Reach, while the expedition retains operating control.

This is the meaning of the programme's controversial ownership clause. The crew may spend ten years building an extraordinary settlement, but the large fixed industrial base is ultimately a corporate asset.

At the end of the ten-year term, operational control of Charter Assets reverts to direct Deep Reach control unless another outcome has been negotiated.

This avoids a legal contradiction: title belongs to Deep Reach throughout; what changes at Year Ten is operational possession and management control.

## 7.6 End-of-Term Options

The baseline contract guarantees only reversion. However, successful settlements are often offered one or more commercial alternatives:

- a renewed ten-year operating charter;
- conversion to a permanent Deep Reach management contract;
- a crew buyout of selected non-strategic assets;
- a profit-sharing joint venture;
- transfer of the crew to a new frontier claim with a fresh charter;
- individual employment within the permanent settlement.

These are offers, not rights. The player cannot assume they will be available.

## 7.7 Right to Leave

No citizen can be contractually imprisoned on a frontier world. Individuals may resign and leave when safe transport exists.

The expedition organisation itself may also abandon the charter, but it may lose its bond, accrued bonuses and claim rights. Deliberate removal or sale of corporate property remains a criminal offence.

## 7.8 Safety Override

Commonwealth safety law overrides production targets. Deep Reach cannot legally demand continued mining if doing so creates an immediate unreasonable threat to life.

The gameplay tension comes from the fact that economic pressure can still make a technically legal choice painful.

# 8. The Player and Settlement Command Structure

The player is the Expedition Director.

Unlike the crash scenario, the player's authority is not derived from emergency survival command. It is created by the charter organisation and recognised by Deep Reach as the principal operational decision-maker.

## 8.1 Expedition Director

The Director is responsible for:

- site strategy;
- settlement construction priorities;
- production targets;
- resource retention versus export;
- supply purchases;
- research priorities;
- expansion decisions;
- staffing policy;
- emergency response;
- contract compliance;
- negotiations with Deep Reach.

## 8.2 Executive Council

A recommended settlement command structure is:

| Position | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Expedition Director | Overall strategic control - the player |
| Operations Chief | Mining, processing and daily production |
| Engineering Chief | Maintenance, power, fabrication and construction |
| Science Chief | Scanning, surveying, analysis and research |
| Logistics Chief | Warehousing, hauling, ship cargo and company manifests |
| Life Support Chief | Water, food, atmosphere, waste and habitation |
| Medical Chief | Health, occupational medicine and emergency care |
| Finance & Contract Officer | Market values, purchases, penalties, accounts and contract status |
| Shipmaster | Independent command of the expedition vessel while in flight |
| Safety Officer | Industrial safety, environmental compliance and incident authority |

The Director cannot legally overrule the Shipmaster on immediate flight-safety decisions or the Safety Officer on a declared life-threatening industrial shutdown without documented review. This reinforces the Commonwealth principle that operational authority remains bounded.

# 9. Expedition Population and Crew Composition

The 500 settlers begin with a very different psychology from the stranded survivors of the Meridian.

They chose the frontier.

They expect hardship, but they also expect profit, achievement or a new life. Many have invested years of savings or accepted deferred compensation tied to charter performance. Their morale is therefore closely connected to whether the settlement appears to be succeeding.

## 9.1 Population Categories

The settlement population contains:

- direct expedition employees;
- members of the expedition cooperative;
- independent specialists under contract;
- ship crew;
- Deep Reach liaison personnel;
- limited family dependants where permitted;
- apprentices and trainees.

## 9.2 Compensation

Workers receive a basic expedition salary plus one or more of:

- production bonuses;
- hazard pay;
- profit-share units;
- discovery bonuses;
- completion bonuses;
- retention bonuses for remaining through the ten-year term.

This gives the player a reason to care about profit beyond a simple score. Financial performance directly affects morale, retention and the settlement's ability to recruit replacements.

## 9.3 Crew Rotation

People are not necessarily trapped on the site for ten years. Visiting vessels may carry personnel to and from the settlement. However, replacement workers cost money, skilled frontier staff are limited, and long-distance routes may make rotations infrequent.

A settlement with poor morale can therefore enter a dangerous spiral: experienced staff leave, replacement costs rise, productivity falls, and the contract becomes harder to fulfil.

# 10. The Expedition Ship

The charter programme requires the crew to arrive with an able vessel.

For the default scenario, the expedition operates the KSV Wayfarer, a privately owned Prospector-class Frontier Utility Vessel.

## 10.1 KSV Wayfarer

| Attribute | Default scenario value |
| --- | --- |
| Role | Expedition transport, orbital survey support, cargo shuttle coordination and emergency evacuation |
| Ownership | Expedition cooperative |
| Interstellar capability | Yes - Vector Exchange Drive |
| Atmospheric capability | Limited; designed for orbital operations and selected prepared landing environments |
| Crew/passenger capacity | 500 in expedition configuration |
| Cargo philosophy | Carries personnel and high-value equipment; bulk corporate starter equipment is delivered by Deep Reach freight |
| Strategic value | The expedition's principal non-corporate capital asset and its ultimate means of leaving the claim |

The Wayfarer matters throughout the scenario. It can:

- perform orbital reconnaissance;
- move specialists between moons or stations;
- relocate high-value equipment;
- transport emergency casualties;
- establish secondary survey sites;
- carry limited high-value cargo;
- move the expedition to another contract if the current one is completed or abandoned.

It is not an unlimited freight solution. Using it for routine bulk haulage is inefficient and consumes expensive reactor, drive and maintenance life.

# 11. The Corporate Settlement Package

Deep Reach's greatest selling point is that a charter crew does not begin with empty hands.

The corporation delivers a Standard Frontier Extraction Package - Class 500, designed to establish a 500-person settlement and support one year of planned industrial operation.

The package is modular because claims vary dramatically.

## 11.1 Habitat and Life Support

The standard package contains:

- pressure-rated modular accommodation;
- communal facilities;
- sanitary systems;
- medical module;
- water purification;
- atmosphere processing for hostile environments;
- waste recovery;
- emergency shelters;
- thermal management;
- environmental sealing equipment.

On a breathable world, some of this equipment becomes reserve capacity. On an airless moon, it is the settlement's lifeline.

## 11.2 Power Package

The standard industrial power package includes:

- one compact modular fusion plant;
- high-density battery storage;
- local distribution equipment;
- backup chemical/fuel generators;
- portable field power units;
- emergency solar or thermal arrays where appropriate.

The fusion plant is powerful enough to begin modern mining, but not powerful enough to support unlimited expansion. Additional generation must eventually be built from local materials or purchased.

## 11.3 Survey and Science Package

- orbital/ship scanner interfaces;
- six levels of deployable scanning capability as equipment becomes installed and calibrated;
- portable scanners;
- survey navigation beacons;
- field sampling equipment;
- environmental monitors;
- geological tools;
- mobile survey vehicles;
- sample storage;
- modular analysis laboratory;
- materials testing equipment.

## 11.4 Construction Package

- autonomous and crewed excavators;
- loaders;
- cranes;
- terrain preparation equipment;
- modular construction frames;
- heavy fastening systems;
- survey-grade foundations;
- structural printers for prepared feedstock;
- welding, cutting and joining systems.

## 11.5 Mining Package

The exact kit depends on the claim, but the standard package contains enough flexibility to begin:

- surface collection;
- shallow excavation;
- drilling;
- crushing;
- loading;
- hauling;
- pumping of liquids;
- capture/compression of gases;
- safe handling of unknown materials.

Highly specialised extraction - deep shafts, extreme-pressure drilling, corrosive brines, molten deposits, cryogenic gases - requires later equipment.

## 11.6 Processing Package

The settlement receives starter modules for:

- crushing and sorting;
- washing;
- thermal separation;
- basic smelting;
- chemical separation;
- gas compression;
- water treatment;
- feedstock preparation;
- common alloy production;
- industrial chemical production.

The package is deliberately broad rather than maximally efficient.

## 11.7 Fabrication Package

The colony receives fabrication equipment capable of making:

- structural members;
- pipework;
- simple mechanical parts;
- housings;
- fasteners;
- cable;
- seals;
- common tools;
- basic circuit substrates;
- simple machine components.

It cannot initially manufacture every advanced component used by its own equipment. High-end sensors, processor modules, Veyrite systems, specialised bearings, medical components and precision power electronics remain imported until the settlement develops the necessary local industrial chains.

This is the scenario's central progression system.

# 12. The One-Year Support Guarantee

Deep Reach guarantees that the supplied settlement package includes enough planned maintenance parts and consumables to sustain normal operation for one year if used within design assumptions.

It is not a magical stockpile that prevents bad decisions.

If the player runs machinery continuously at maximum load, crashes vehicles, expands far beyond the intended starter settlement or ignores maintenance, the one-year reserve can be consumed far earlier.

## 12.1 What the Guarantee Covers

The package includes approximately one year of forecast requirements for:

- machine filters;
- lubricants;
- seals;
- common bearings;
- cutting heads;
- drill components;
- tyres/tracks;
- coolant additives;
- fusion-plant service components;
- electrical protection modules;
- atmosphere filters;
- medical consumables;
- sanitation chemicals;
- laboratory consumables;
- food reserve supplements;
- critical fabrication tooling;
- common electronics replacements.

## 12.2 What It Does Not Cover

The guarantee does not promise replacement of:

- destroyed machinery;
- major accident damage;
- recklessly overused equipment;
- lost cargo;
- unplanned settlement expansion;
- strategic components outside normal wear forecasts;
- construction of secondary colonies;
- arbitrary luxury imports.

## 12.3 The Year-One Objective

The real objective is not simply to reach Day 365 with supplies remaining.

By the end of Year One the settlement should be able to locally sustain:

- food;
- water;
- breathable atmosphere where required;
- power;
- common structural material;
- common machine parts;
- industrial lubricants or substitutes;
- basic electrical conductors;
- simple electronics;
- common construction feedstocks;
- routine medical supplies where practical;
- at least one locally maintainable extraction chain;
- at least one profitable export chain.

The more of these the colony controls, the less vulnerable it is to company freight pricing.

# 13. Selecting and Claiming a Mining Site

The scenario can begin with a fixed authored claim or a procedurally selected charter location.

Deep Reach classifies claims into three broad tiers.

## 13.1 Core Reach Claim - Easier Start

Located inside the Koplin system, usually on an established moon, asteroid region or marginal planetary zone.

Advantages:

- shorter supply routes;
- frequent company vessels;
- better maps;
- known environmental conditions;
- easier rescue;
- mature communications.

Disadvantages:

- lower average deposit richness;
- more exhausted resources;
- greater competition;
- higher charter fees;
- less discovery bonus potential.

## 13.2 Outer Reach Claim - Standard Start

Located in the outer Koplin system or on a poorly developed body.

Advantages:

- good resource potential;
- moderate support distance;
- meaningful unexplored territory;
- fewer competing settlements.

Disadvantages:

- longer resupply windows;
- incomplete surveys;
- harsher environmental uncertainty;
- higher logistics cost.

## 13.3 Beyond-Koplin Claim - Hard Start

Located in one of the nearby systems reachable by Vector Exchange travel.

Advantages:

- untouched resource fields;
- highest discovery premiums;
- possibility of rare substances;
- potential first-mover control of entire regional supply chains.

Disadvantages:

- long freight cycles;
- expensive emergency support;
- limited communications;
- incomplete maps;
- greater biological/geological uncertainty;
- much higher cost for imported consumables.

## 13.4 Claim Boundaries

A charter does not automatically grant ownership of an entire planet. Deep Reach registers defined extraction zones or bodies under Commonwealth frontier law.

The player may discover resources outside the original claim. They can then:

- request claim expansion;
- purchase an adjacent charter right;
- establish a survey reservation;
- report a strategic discovery to the Commonwealth;
- leave the deposit unworked.

Attempting to secretly extract outside the claim can create legal disputes and penalties.

# 14. Arrival and Settlement Establishment

The opening is organised and professional rather than catastrophic.

The Wayfarer reaches the assigned body and performs final orbital validation. Deep Reach freight modules have either arrived shortly beforehand or travel in convoy with the expedition.

The first challenge is choosing the exact settlement position.

A poor site can make the next ten years unnecessarily difficult.

The player balances:

- known resource signatures;
- flat construction ground;
- water access;
- radiation exposure;
- temperature;
- atmospheric hazards;
- access to sunlight;
- distance to landing zones;
- terrain difficulty;
- expansion space;
- geological stability;
- travel distance to initial deposits.

The first permanent structures are not improvised huts. They are corporate modules deployed from cargo frames. However, the player must immediately start replacing imported dependence with locally sourced infrastructure.

## 14.1 Recommended First-30-Day Priorities

1. Establish main power and emergency backup.
2. Bring life support and medical modules online.
3. Confirm water reserves and local water strategy.
4. Establish the logistics yard and warehouse.
5. Deploy the analysis laboratory.
6. Calibrate local scanners.
7. Survey the immediate colony sector.
8. Identify at least one structural material source.
9. Identify at least one energy/fuel or power-expansion path.
10. Identify at least one commercially attractive export substance.
11. Begin local food or long-term food-support planning.
12. Establish maintenance workshops.
13. Start producing the simplest high-consumption replacement parts.

The first month is therefore about removing unknowns.

# 15. Scanning, Surveying and Sample Analysis

This scenario gives the game's scanning and surveying systems a direct economic purpose from the first hour.

The player is not scanning merely to survive. They are searching for profitable industrial opportunities.

## 15.1 Scanning

Orbital and surface scans identify probability fields rather than perfect resource labels.

A scan may reveal:

- conductive anomaly;
- dense structural mineral signature;
- volatile concentration;
- carbon-rich layer;
- magnetic material probability;
- saline/brine body;
- unusual biological concentration;
- thermal vent;
- crystalline high-pressure formation.

Higher scanner levels improve:

- spatial resolution;
- depth;
- material classification confidence;
- ability to separate overlapping deposits;
- detection of rare signatures.

## 15.2 Surveying

A survey turns a broad scan result into a practical mining target.

Survey teams determine:

- access;
- terrain;
- deposit boundaries;
- sampling locations;
- initial extraction difficulty;
- environmental hazards;
- likely quantity;
- likely depth;
- transport distance.

## 15.3 Samples

Samples are physical evidence. They enter the Samples & Analysis system and are prioritised against commercial or operational needs.

A sample may be analysed for:

- composition;
- purity;
- structural strength;
- conductivity;
- magnetism;
- thermal properties;
- fuel value;
- chemical reactivity;
- corrosion risk;
- toxicity;
- biological activity;
- processing difficulty;
- commercial rarity.

## 15.4 Commercial Discovery

Once a substance is fully analysed, it receives its generated substance identity and properties. The settlement's market system then compares those properties with Deep Reach demand.

This creates an important distinction:

A visually ordinary rock can be economically extraordinary, while an impressive-looking crystal can be almost worthless.

# 16. The Substance Economy

The Deep Reach scenario should make the game's substance model the centre of the economy.

A substance's value is not based only on a fixed category. It is calculated from a combination of:

- property usefulness;
- purity;
- abundance;
- extraction cost;
- processing cost;
- current market demand;
- transport density;
- hazard level;
- strategic restrictions;
- local scarcity within Commonwealth industry.

## 16.1 Quality Grades

For trade purposes Deep Reach uses five commercial grades.

| Grade | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| D | Low-value or heavily contaminated material; usually useful only locally or after processing |
| C | Standard industrial material |
| B | Good commercial quality |
| A | High purity or unusually strong property profile |
| S | Exceptional, strategic or scientifically unusual material requiring individual contract valuation |

A deposit can therefore become more valuable through processing even if its raw material grade is poor.

## 16.2 Market Demand

Market demand changes over time.

Examples:

- a major orbital construction programme increases structural-metal prices;
- Veyrite-drive production increases demand for specialised magnetic and conductive materials;
- an agricultural disease raises prices for a biochemical precursor;
- a new refining process reduces demand for one catalyst and increases demand for another;
- wartime or emergency stockpiling creates temporary premiums.

The settlement should not be able to rely forever on one profitable material.

## 16.3 Unknown Substance Bonuses

Deep Reach pays discovery bonuses for materials not previously catalogued in its commercial database, especially if they possess unusual properties.

The Commonwealth science service may also become involved if a discovery has scientific significance beyond its market value.

# 17. Mining and Extraction Operations

Mining begins with matching the right extraction method to the deposit.

## 17.1 Surface Collection

Suitable for loose rock, exposed mineral, biological material and easily accessible deposits.

Advantages:

- cheap;
- low infrastructure;
- quick startup.

Disadvantages:

- often limited quantity;
- high transport labour;
- low long-term scalability.

## 17.2 Open Excavation

Used for shallow deposits and large exposed seams.

Requires:

- excavation machinery;
- haul roads;
- loaders;
- dust control;
- slope management;
- drainage where relevant.

## 17.3 Underground Mining

Used when valuable material lies beneath significant overburden.

Requires:

- shafts or declines;
- ventilation;
- structural support;
- pumping;
- emergency refuge;
- specialised survey data.

## 17.4 Liquid Extraction

Used for brines, hydrocarbons, subsurface water and chemical reservoirs.

Requires:

- wells;
- pumps;
- seals;
- storage;
- corrosion-resistant pipework;
- contamination control.

## 17.5 Gas Extraction

Used for industrial gases or volatile deposits.

Requires pressure-rated systems and leak control.

## 17.6 Biological Harvesting

Where local law and biology permit it, naturally occurring organisms may contain commercially useful substances.

Biological harvesting has unique constraints:

- regeneration rate;
- ecological impact;
- contamination;
- seasonality;
- genetic variation;
- preservation requirements.

A high-value biological substance can create a completely different mining settlement: less excavation, more cultivation, controlled harvesting and biochemical processing.

# 18. Processing, Refining and Manufacturing

The player has three basic choices for every resource:

1. Export it raw.
2. Process it before export.
3. Retain it as part of the local industrial economy.

Raw export produces money quickly but ships low-value waste and leaves the settlement dependent on imports.

Processing requires energy and machinery but improves value density and creates useful by-products.

Local manufacturing may produce no immediate sale at all, but it reduces future dependency.

This creates the core economic tension of the scenario.

## 18.1 Example Decision

A newly discovered conductive ore could be:

- exported immediately as Grade C raw ore;
- refined into Grade A conductor feedstock for a much higher sale value;
- converted into cable and motor windings needed to expand local machinery;
- split between all three uses.

The best short-term financial choice may be the worst Year-Two survival choice.

## 18.2 Industrial Depth

Self-sufficiency is measured by industrial depth, not by whether the colony owns a fabricator.

To make a replacement pump, the settlement might need:

- structural alloy;
- seals;
- lubricant;
- conductor wire;
- magnetic material;
- control electronics;
- sensor elements;
- machine tooling;
- calibration.

Each layer becomes a production chain the player can eventually localise.

# 19. Power, Water, Food and Life Support

The settlement starts with these systems functioning, but the scenario makes their long-term cost visible.

## 19.1 Power

The corporate fusion plant is the initial backbone. Its advantages are high output and reliability. Its weakness is imported maintenance dependency.

The player is encouraged to diversify into locally maintainable generation:

- solar;
- wind where atmosphere permits;
- hydro where geography permits;
- geothermal;
- combustion using local fuel;
- thermal systems;
- later locally supported fusion infrastructure.

The right solution depends on the world.

## 19.2 Water

Water may be:

- abundant surface freshwater;
- ice;
- saline ocean;
- subsurface brine;
- chemically bound in minerals;
- almost absent.

Water is not only a drinking resource. It is also required by agriculture, industry, cooling, chemical processing and sometimes fuel production.

## 19.3 Food

The expedition arrives with one year of baseline food support combined between stored reserves and compact controlled-environment agriculture modules.

The goal is to convert that into a local sustainable food economy.

Possible strategies include:

- enclosed hydroponics;
- local agriculture where safe;
- imported seed stocks;
- cultured protein;
- controlled livestock systems;
- approved local biological food sources after analysis.

## 19.4 Air

On a habitable planet, atmosphere processing may be minimal. On a hostile moon, breathable gas becomes an industrial commodity and a major part of settlement cost.

# 20. Maintenance and Industrial Self-Sufficiency

Maintenance is the scenario's hidden economy.

A settlement can appear profitable while slowly consuming the machines that make its profit possible.

Every major machine has:

- operating hours;
- wear state;
- maintenance intervals;
- required consumables;
- replaceable components;
- failure risk.

## 20.1 Maintenance Levels

Level 1 - Consumable replacement

Filters, fluids, seals and simple wear items.

Level 2 - Workshop repair

Bearings, motors, cables, pumps, structural damage and replaceable modules.

Level 3 - Local component manufacture

The colony can manufacture common replacement parts from local materials.

Level 4 - Advanced component manufacture

Sensors, high-performance electronics, specialist alloys, medical parts and advanced control systems.

Level 5 - Strategic independence

The colony can maintain most of its industrial base without routine imported parts.

The player's ideal Year-One outcome is not Level 5. It is to reach enough of Levels 2-3 that imported components become manageable rather than existential.

## 20.2 Self-Sufficiency Score

The scenario can track a settlement-wide Self-Sufficiency Index based on how much of the previous year's consumption could be replaced locally.

Suggested categories:

- food;
- water;
- power;
- construction;
- mechanical parts;
- electrical parts;
- industrial chemicals;
- medical consumables;
- machine tooling;
- transport fuel;
- life-support consumables.

This index becomes one of the clearest measures of progress.

# 21. Company Collection and Supply Vessels

Deep Reach vessels are the settlement's physical connection to the wider economy.

They serve two purposes at once:

1. collect contracted material;
2. deliver ordered supplies.

## 21.1 Service Frequency

Baseline service depends on distance.

| Location | Typical service cadence |
| --- | --- |
| Inner/mid Koplin system | Every 60-90 days |
| Outer Koplin system | Every 90-120 days |
| Nearby interstellar claim | Every 150-210 days |
| High-risk frontier route | Individually scheduled; often only 2 visits per year |

A scheduled visit is not guaranteed to arrive to the exact day. Weather, orbital mechanics, mechanical failures and route priorities can create delays.

## 21.2 Manifest Process

Before arrival, the player prepares:

Outbound manifest

- material;
- quantity;
- quality;
- storage condition;
- declared hazards.

Inbound order

- spare parts;
- consumables;
- machinery;
- food;
- medical supplies;
- advanced components;
- personal goods;
- construction modules.

The vessel calculates net settlement value after purchase and sale.

## 21.3 Emergency Deliveries

Priority freight can be requested, but it is extremely expensive. Interstellar emergency freight may cost more than the original value of the missing component.

This creates meaningful consequences for poor planning without making a single mistake automatically fatal.

# 22. Trade, Prices, Credits and Barter

The scenario introduces a formal monetary economy.

The standard unit is the Commonwealth Credit (CC).

## 22.1 Settlement Account

The charter settlement has a Deep Reach commercial account containing:

- credit balance;
- outstanding orders;
- freight charges;
- contractual bonuses;
- penalties;
- bond balance;
- accrued worker profit share;
- insurance claims;
- market-adjustment credits.

## 22.2 Direct Exchange

When a collection vessel visits, the settlement may exchange cargo directly against incoming goods.

For example:

- 2,000 CC of refined conductive material delivered;
- 1,300 CC of replacement components purchased;
- 200 CC freight charge;
- 500 CC net credit added to the settlement account.

This can be presented in gameplay as barter while still using credits underneath so values remain transparent.

## 22.3 Pricing

The sale price can be modelled as:

Base market value x quality modifier x demand modifier x contract modifier - handling adjustments

Imported goods are:

Core market price + freight + risk surcharge + priority surcharge where applicable

The result is intentionally asymmetric. Remote settlements can earn huge premiums for rare material, but imported low-value bulk goods become uneconomical.

## 22.4 Why Self-Sufficiency Matters Economically

The company can sell a remote colony a tonne of structural steel. The question is whether it makes sense to spend interstellar cargo capacity transporting something the colony could produce itself from local ore.

The deeper the frontier, the more the economy naturally rewards local manufacturing.

# 23. Contract Performance and Corporate Ratings

Deep Reach evaluates each charter continuously.

## 23.1 Major Metrics

| Metric | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Production Reliability | Does the settlement consistently produce exportable material? |
| Commercial Value | Total market value delivered |
| Quality | Average grade and contamination rate |
| Safety | Injury, fatality and major incident performance |
| Asset Condition | How well corporate equipment is maintained |
| Self-Sufficiency | How dependent the site remains on routine supply |
| Environmental Compliance | Whether extraction follows permit conditions |
| Financial Standing | Debt, unpaid orders and cash reserve |
| Discovery Value | New materials, deposits or commercially important properties identified |

## 23.2 Charter Rating

Suggested ratings:

- AAA - Exemplary Frontier Operator
- AA - High Reliability
- A - Strong
- B - Acceptable
- C - At Risk
- D - Remediation Required
- E - Contract Breach / Closure Review

Higher ratings unlock:

- better credit limits;
- lower freight surcharges;
- earlier access to advanced equipment;
- additional claim offers;
- negotiated contract improvements;
- priority on replacement personnel.

Poor ratings make dependency worse precisely when the colony is struggling.

# 24. The Ten-Year Operational Arc

The charter is long enough that the settlement should change dramatically.

## Year 1 - Establishment

Primary question: Can the settlement become viable before free support ends?

Key activities:

- deploy corporate equipment;
- discover initial deposits;
- establish profitable extraction;
- build local food/water/power resilience;
- create basic repair manufacturing;
- deliver first commercial cargoes.

## Year 2 - The Support Cliff

The company stops providing routine free maintenance supplies.

This is the first major difficulty spike.

The player discovers whether Year-One decisions were genuinely sustainable or merely productive.

## Years 3-4 - Optimisation

The settlement should now have stable basic industry.

Focus shifts toward:

- better refining;
- higher material grades;
- automation;
- second deposits;
- expanded power;
- improved housing;
- deeper local manufacturing.

## Years 5-6 - Expansion

The original deposit may no longer be enough.

The player considers:

- satellite mines;
- long-haul local logistics;
- orbital extraction;
- adjacent claims;
- secondary settlements.

## Years 7-8 - Strategic Production

The strongest settlements begin producing advanced materials rather than merely mining ore.

Deep Reach may offer special contracts for:

- rare catalysts;
- Veyrite-adjacent materials;
- high-purity conductors;
- advanced ceramics;
- biological compounds;
- difficult deep deposits.

## Year 9 - Reversion Planning

The end of the charter becomes economically real.

The player must decide what they want next.

Possible strategies:

- maximise final profit;
- negotiate renewal;
- prepare the crew to depart;
- buy portable equipment;
- move to a new claim;
- seek a joint venture;
- position key staff for permanent employment.

## Year 10 - Completion

The settlement undergoes final audit.

Deep Reach checks:

- asset condition;
- environmental obligations;
- outstanding debt;
- unresolved claims;
- inventory ownership;
- contract resource accounting.

If the contract is completed successfully, the crew receives its completion bonus and the charter's fixed corporate assets revert to direct company management unless a new agreement replaces the baseline terms.

# 25. Expansion Through the Koplin System

A successful settlement does not need to remain a single mine.

The player may use profits to establish:

- remote extraction camps;
- automated collection sites;
- processing hubs;
- orbital warehouses;
- fuel depots;
- scientific stations;
- small dependent settlements.

The Koplin system has the advantage of dense infrastructure. Existing stations can provide repair, medicine and transport support. However, many high-value easy deposits are already claimed.

The player therefore competes less on discovery and more on efficiency, processing and the ability to exploit difficult material economically.

# 26. Expansion Beyond Koplin

Interstellar expansion changes the scale of the scenario.

A nearby system may take months to reach. Freight cycles become strategic planning events rather than routine deliveries.

A player who expands beyond Koplin needs:

- Veyrite-capable ship support;
- interstellar navigation;
- larger inventory buffers;
- more autonomous medicine;
- deeper local manufacturing;
- stronger emergency reserves;
- communications planning;
- duplicated critical equipment.

## 26.1 Interstellar Supply Philosophy

In-system settlements can survive by ordering missing parts.

Interstellar settlements survive by predicting which parts they will need before the next ship departs.

That difference should be felt directly in gameplay.

## 26.2 Frontier Discovery

Beyond Koplin, the player may find:

- unusual geologies;
- untouched metal-rich bodies;
- alien microbial ecosystems;
- unfamiliar plant-like life;
- extreme high-pressure minerals;
- rare isotopes;
- anomalous Veyrite-associated formations.

The scenario should stop short of revealing the designer-only truth behind the anomaly unless the wider game's campaign deliberately connects the scenarios.

# 27. Workforce, Morale and Settlement Society

This is a commercial community, not a military camp.

People expect:

- reasonable housing;
- fair work rotations;
- safety;
- medical care;
- privacy;
- communication with home;
- recreation;
- transparent compensation;
- some influence over life outside direct industrial operations.

## 27.1 Morale Drivers

Positive:

- profitable quarters;
- safety records;
- comfortable housing;
- bonuses;
- successful discoveries;
- reliable food;
- contact with home;
- visible progress toward self-sufficiency.

Negative:

- repeated equipment failure;
- excessive overtime;
- corporate debt;
- delayed supply ships;
- accidents;
- poor food variety;
- unsafe mining targets;
- perceived unfair bonuses;
- being forced to export resources the settlement needs.

## 27.2 Settlement Governance

The Expedition Director controls operations, but civilian life gradually requires local institutions.

By later years the settlement may form:

- elected resident council;
- workplace safety committee;
- education service;
- recreation organisations;
- cultural groups;
- dispute mediation;
- local commercial services.

This creates a settlement that feels inhabited rather than merely operated.

# 28. AI, Automation and Robotics

Deep Reach uses extensive automation but remains bound by post-AI-war law.

## 28.1 Allowed Uses

AI can:

- optimise haul routes;
- analyse samples;
- predict equipment failure;
- schedule maintenance;
- model deposits;
- recommend production plans;
- operate bounded mining machinery;
- manage warehouse movement;
- assist medical diagnosis.

## 28.2 Restricted Uses

AI cannot independently:

- change the settlement's strategic objectives;
- commit the expedition to a new claim;
- enter financial contracts;
- decide to conceal production;
- command unrestricted security systems;
- replicate itself into uncontrolled hardware;
- remove biological oversight from life-critical systems.

## 28.3 Automation Trade-off

Automation reduces workforce demand but increases dependence on:

- sensors;
- processors;
- actuators;
- calibration;
- clean power;
- specialised replacement parts.

A less automated mine may require more people but be easier to maintain locally.

This should be a genuine design choice rather than a simple technology upgrade.

# 29. Environmental Law and Biological Resources

The Commonwealth's frontier rules do not permit unrestricted destruction simply because a world is uninhabited by intelligent life.

## 29.1 Barren Worlds

Mining regulation focuses on:

- physical safety;
- orbital debris;
- contamination of scientific sites;
- long-term structural hazards;
- protection of strategic materials.

## 29.2 Living Worlds

Any world with indigenous biology receives stronger controls.

Before commercial harvesting, the settlement must determine:

- whether the organism is native;
- whether it is rare;
- ecological role;
- reproduction rate;
- toxicity;
- contamination risk;
- sustainable harvest rate.

## 29.3 Biological Substance Market

Deep Reach Biochem may offer enormous premiums for compounds with useful properties.

Possible discoveries include:

- natural lubricants;
- extreme-temperature polymers;
- medicinal compounds;
- catalysts;
- structural fibres;
- radiation-resistant pigments;
- enzymes;
- high-energy biological oils.

The player can therefore operate a commercially successful bio-extraction settlement without conventional metal mining being its main business.

# 30. Hazards, Emergencies and Rescue

The settlement is connected to civilisation, but distance still kills.

## 30.1 Industrial Hazards

- mine collapse;
- gas release;
- dust explosion;
- equipment collision;
- pressure failure;
- toxic exposure;
- fire;
- electrical failure;
- reactor fault;
- chemical spill.

## 30.2 Environmental Hazards

- extreme storms;
- temperature events;
- seismic activity;
- radiation storms;
- meteor impacts;
- flooding;
- landslides;
- corrosive atmosphere;
- unknown biology.

## 30.3 Logistics Hazards

- delayed freighter;
- wrong replacement part;
- damaged cargo;
- route closure;
- ship maintenance issue;
- insufficient warehouse capacity;
- unplanned population movement.

## 30.4 Rescue

In the Koplin system, emergency help may arrive in days or weeks.

At an interstellar claim, rescue may take months.

The company therefore requires remote settlements to maintain emergency reserves even when doing so appears financially inefficient.

# 31. Competitors, Claims and Corporate Politics

Deep Reach is large, but it is not the only frontier company.

Other organisations may operate nearby:

- rival mining corporations;
- independent prospectors;
- Commonwealth science missions;
- settlement cooperatives;
- transport companies;
- local governments;
- strategic-material authorities.

## 31.1 Competition Without Warfare

The Commonwealth does not tolerate companies fighting private wars over claims.

Competition instead appears through:

- bidding;
- survey races;
- claim challenges;
- labour recruitment;
- pricing;
- transport access;
- patents;
- political lobbying;
- accusations of environmental damage;
- attempts to purchase a successful charter.

## 31.2 Deep Reach Internal Politics

The corporation itself is not a single personality.

Different divisions may want different things from the player's settlement.

Frontier Services may prioritise contract stability. Strategic Materials may demand rapid exploitation of a rare deposit. Logistics may refuse an inefficient route. Corporate finance may tighten credit. Research may want samples preserved rather than mined.

This creates narrative pressure without requiring a single villainous executive.

# 32. Research and Technology Progression

The scenario should not reuse the crash scenario's technology progression unchanged.

The crew already understands Year-5326 science.

Research represents local industrial adaptation, not rediscovery of known physics.

## 32.1 Research Categories

Local Materials Engineering

Determine how discovered substances can replace imported materials.

Extraction Engineering

Develop equipment for deeper, hotter, more corrosive or more dangerous deposits.

Processing

Improve purity, yield and by-product recovery.

Manufacturing Independence

Move from simple replacement parts to advanced components.

Power

Adapt generation to local resources and eventually build locally maintainable high-output systems.

Biology

Understand local ecosystems, food potential and biochemical resources.

Automation

Improve efficiency while managing maintenance complexity.

Logistics

Reduce haulage cost, improve storage, optimise cargo and eventually build local orbital infrastructure.

Veyrite Systems

Late-game frontier research can include maintenance, detection and supporting materials for Vector Exchange technology, but actual Veyrite drive manufacture remains extremely demanding.

## 32.2 Research Inputs

Research should consume more than abstract points.

Advanced projects may require:

- analysed samples;
- test material;
- prototype components;
- specialist equipment;
- power;
- researcher time;
- imported calibration standards.

This tightly connects research to the mining economy.

# 33. Scenario Gameplay Progression

The scenario can be understood as seven overlapping stages.

## Stage 1 - Deploy

Goal: make the corporate starter settlement operational.

Player experience:

- unload modules;
- connect power;
- establish logistics;
- verify initial supplies;
- choose exact colony position.

## Stage 2 - Discover

Goal: understand the claim.

Player experience:

- scan;
- survey;
- collect samples;
- analyse properties;
- identify commercial targets.

## Stage 3 - Extract

Goal: create first saleable production.

Player experience:

- open first mine;
- establish hauling;
- manage workers;
- control storage;
- meet first collection vessel.

## Stage 4 - Localise

Goal: survive the end of free support.

Player experience:

- produce local construction materials;
- make replacement parts;
- establish food and water resilience;
- add locally maintainable power;
- reduce imported consumables.

## Stage 5 - Optimise

Goal: become genuinely profitable.

Player experience:

- refine resources;
- automate;
- improve grades;
- reduce energy cost;
- exploit by-products.

## Stage 6 - Expand

Goal: prevent dependence on one deposit.

Player experience:

- secondary mines;
- new sectors;
- additional claims;
- orbital sites;
- possibly new worlds.

## Stage 7 - Complete the Charter

Goal: finish Year Ten in strong financial and operational condition.

Player experience:

- negotiate end-state;
- prepare asset audit;
- decide what the crew takes with them;
- choose renewal, departure or next frontier contract.

# 34. Failure States and Recovery Paths

The scenario should rarely end from one mistake. Failure should usually be a deteriorating economic or industrial condition the player can see approaching.

## 34.1 Insolvency

The colony cannot pay for essential imports.

Recovery:

- emergency credit;
- sell stored high-value material;
- reduce non-critical operations;
- accept a less favourable corporate rescue package;
- surrender part of future bonuses.

## 34.2 Maintenance Collapse

Too many machines fail because local manufacturing was neglected.

Recovery:

- cannibalise idle machines;
- buy emergency parts;
- simplify production;
- switch to less automated methods;
- concentrate on one profitable extraction chain.

## 34.3 Food or Life-Support Failure

Recovery may require expensive emergency supply, rationing or partial evacuation.

## 34.4 Contract Breach

Examples:

- deliberate off-contract resource sales;
- major falsified manifests;
- repeated environmental violations;
- illegal removal of corporate assets.

Consequences can include:

- fines;
- rating loss;
- forced audit;
- removal of Director authority;
- charter termination.

## 34.5 Site Failure

A claim may simply prove economically poor.

A competent player should be able to recognise this and petition Deep Reach to relocate before total collapse. Relocation is costly but preferable to pretending every seed must contain a rich deposit near the start point.

# 35. Completion, Victory and Post-Contract Outcomes

The scenario is successfully completed when the settlement reaches the end of Year Ten without unresolved critical breach and the final asset audit is completed.

Success is graded rather than binary.

## 35.1 Completion Tiers

Survival Completion

The charter is completed, but profits are modest and the settlement remains heavily dependent on imports.

Commercial Success

Strong profit, reliable exports and acceptable self-sufficiency.

Frontier Success

The colony is deeply self-sufficient, operates multiple extraction chains and has expanded beyond the original claim.

Strategic Success

The settlement discovers or produces substances of major Commonwealth importance and becomes a critical Deep Reach operation.

Exceptional Charter

Top-tier safety, profitability, discovery, workforce retention and asset condition. The crew becomes one of the most sought-after frontier operators in the Commonwealth.

## 35.2 Post-Contract Choices

At completion the player may be offered:

- Renew - continue operating the settlement under a revised contract.
- Join Deep Reach - convert the operation into permanent corporate management.
- Buy Selected Assets - purchase eligible non-strategic facilities and create an independent settlement.
- Take a New Charter - transfer the crew and ship to a new claim.
- Return to Koplin - end the expedition and realise accumulated earnings.
- Continue Sandbox - keep playing under a negotiated post-contract arrangement.

The original contract's default remains that corporate Charter Assets return to direct Deep Reach control at Year Ten.

# 36. Opening Narrative Sequence

The scenario should open as a deliberate contrast to the Meridian disaster.

## 36.1 Recruitment Broadcast

The player first sees a polished Deep Reach recruitment transmission.

It shows enormous asteroid excavators, clean frontier habitats, glittering orbital refineries and crews standing beneath unfamiliar skies.

The message emphasises:

- independence;
- high earnings;
- exploration;
- company-provided infrastructure;
- ten years to build something extraordinary.

The small legal text is visible, but nobody in the advertisement reads it aloud.

## 36.2 Contract Briefing

The player is then shown the real terms.

A corporate contract officer explains the non-negotiable foundations:

- one year of included planned support;
- ten-year exclusive resource sale;
- corporate ownership of charter assets;
- market-linked payment;
- regular collection and supply service;
- requirement to comply with Commonwealth safety and environmental law.

The player signs as Expedition Director.

## 36.3 Departure

The 500-person expedition departs Koplin aboard the Wayfarer while Deep Reach freight travels on the assigned route.

Unlike the crash scenario, the tone is hopeful. The crew has chosen this.

## 36.4 Arrival

The Wayfarer enters orbit around the charter body.

The first scan reveals known corporate survey markers but leaves much of the local geology unresolved.

The Deep Reach liaison transmits the first operational objective:

Establish the settlement. Confirm one viable export substance. Prepare a certified outbound cargo before the first collection vessel arrives.

## 36.5 First Strategic Choice

Before a building is placed, the player must decide whether to establish near:

- the safest terrain;
- the strongest preliminary resource signal;
- the best water access;
- the best long-term expansion area.

The scenario begins with opportunity rather than disaster, but the same fundamental game principle applies: location matters.

# 37. Example Starting Claim

The default authored version of the scenario can use Koplin 7 - Moon D, Charter Block K7-D-41, known informally as Greywake.

Greywake is an outer-system moon with a thin, unbreathable atmosphere, extensive ice deposits, old impact basins and incomplete mineral surveys.

## 37.1 Why Greywake Works as the Default

It is far enough from Koplin 3 that logistics matter, but still inside the home system so the player learns the contract economy before interstellar distances become dominant.

It contains:

- accessible water ice;
- common silicates;
- several metallic signatures;
- carbon-bearing deposits;
- suspected deep brine;
- at least one unknown high-density mineral anomaly.

It lacks:

- breathable atmosphere;
- native food;
- easy surface biomass;
- large established infrastructure.

The player therefore receives advanced equipment but must build a genuine industrial settlement.

## 37.2 Initial Corporate Knowledge

Deep Reach's pre-charter survey identifies three promising sectors but does not fully analyse them.

Sector A - Basin Rim

Strong structural mineral signal, excellent building terrain, moderate distance from ice.

Sector B - Fracture Zone

Higher conductive/magnetic probability, difficult terrain, possible deep volatile pockets.

Sector C - Polar Shelf

Excellent water ice, weak initial metal signatures, strong solar-season variation.

There is no obvious perfect start.

## 37.3 Hidden Opportunity

One deeper anomaly contains an unusually pure magnetic-conductive composite mineral with potential value in advanced motor and drive-support manufacturing. It is not required for survival. Discovering it rewards thorough scanning and analysis rather than simply following the most obvious starting deposit.

# 39. Canon Rules for Future Development

The following rules should be treated as scenario canon unless deliberately revised.

1. The scenario occurs in the normal Commonwealth frontier. There is no anomaly displacement.
2. The three species remain socially integrated. Crew conflict should not default to species hostility.
3. Deep Reach is powerful but not sovereign. Commonwealth law remains above the corporation.
4. The player is a voluntary charter operator, not a slave or prisoner. Leaving is legally possible, though financially painful.
5. The initial one-year package is genuinely capable. The company is not secretly sabotaging the crew.
6. The difficulty comes from dependency and logistics. Maintenance, freight and local industrial depth are central.
7. Knowledge is not the bottleneck. The crew knows modern science; materials, machinery, labour, energy and supply chains are the bottlenecks.
8. The company buys properties, not just named ores. The substance system should drive commercial value.
9. The settlement may retain resources needed for local survival and industry. Exclusivity applies to external sale.
10. All fixed Charter Assets remain legally Deep Reach property during the term. The settlement has operational control.
11. At Year Ten, direct operational control reverts to Deep Reach by default. Any buyout, renewal or joint venture is a negotiated alternative.
12. The expedition's pre-existing ship remains independent property unless separately financed.
13. Supply vessels both collect and deliver. Trade should feel physical and scheduled rather than an instant market menu.
14. Freight cost increases with distance. Interstellar settlements are pushed toward deeper self-sufficiency.
15. No start location is guaranteed to contain every important resource nearby. Trade, adaptation and relocation are legitimate strategies.
16. A poor claim should be difficult, not automatically impossible. The player may negotiate relocation or purchase critical inputs.
17. Automation creates maintenance dependency. More advanced is not automatically more resilient.
18. Environmental rules matter on living worlds. Biological discoveries can be valuable but cannot be harvested without limits.
19. The ten-year term should change gameplay over time. Year One, Years 2-4, Years 5-8 and Years 9-10 should feel strategically different.
20. Successful completion should open future play rather than simply end the save. Renewal, new charters and sandbox continuation are valid outcomes.

# Scenario Design Summary

The Deep Reach Mining Charter is deliberately not a second survival-from-nothing scenario.

The player begins with exactly the kind of equipment an advanced Year-5326 civilisation should be able to provide. The challenge is that advanced machinery creates a supply-chain obligation. The first year gives the player time to understand the world, establish profitable extraction and replace imported dependency with local industry. After that, every replacement component imported from Koplin is a reminder that the settlement has not yet solved that part of its economy.

The ten-year contract then gives the colony a reason to keep growing after basic survival has been secured. More output creates more money. More money permits more equipment. More equipment creates more maintenance demand. Richer deposits encourage expansion. Expansion increases logistics. Higher-value processing increases energy and industrial complexity. The settlement therefore evolves from a supplied mining camp into a genuine frontier industrial society.

The company is both partner and pressure source. Deep Reach wants the colony to survive because a dead settlement produces nothing. It wants the colony to become efficient because efficient settlements generate high-value cargo. But it also benefits when the settlement remains dependent on company freight and remains legally tied to corporate assets.

That tension provides the scenario's central long-term question:

At the end of ten years, have you merely built an excellent Deep Reach mine - or have you built a crew, economy and industrial base capable of choosing what comes next?
