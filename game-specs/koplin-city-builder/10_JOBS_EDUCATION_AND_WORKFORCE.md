# 10 — Jobs, Education and Workforce

## Workforce model

Employment links citizens to organisations/buildings and is constrained by qualifications, accessibility, income, working conditions and labour demand.

KCB-WORK-001 — Jobs MUST be attached to actual workplaces or explicitly off-map/remote employment.

KCB-WORK-002 — A building’s theoretical job capacity is distinct from filled jobs.

## Job attributes

Each job category defines sector, qualification requirement, schedule profile, income band, remote-work eligibility, workplace occupancy requirement, automation exposure and critical-service flag.

Examples include care, education, engineering, research, administration, retail/service, construction, logistics, maintenance, manufacturing, culture and emergency response.

KCB-WORK-010 — Species MUST NOT restrict job eligibility.

## Matching

Worker-to-job matching considers qualification, travel cost, wage, existing job stability, schedule fit, accessibility needs and unemployment duration.

KCB-WORK-020 — Employers prefer suitable nearby workers but may recruit farther when labour is scarce.

KCB-WORK-021 — Workers may change jobs when a materially better option persists, with friction/cooldowns.

## Unemployment

Headline unemployment is decomposed into:
- frictional;
- qualification mismatch;
- geographic/access mismatch;
- cyclical/demand;
- voluntary/non-participation.

KCB-WORK-030 — The UI MUST identify which form dominates.

KCB-WORK-031 — Adding generic jobs is not presented as the solution when the issue is qualification or transport mismatch.

## Automation

Year-5300 technology means many routine tasks are automated.

KCB-WORK-040 — Automation changes labour intensity, not whether a workplace exists.

KCB-WORK-041 — Highly automated facilities still require accountable operators, maintainers, engineers, inspectors or service staff as appropriate.

KCB-WORK-042 — Automation upgrades may trade capital, energy and maintenance demand for lower staffing demand.

## Education system

Education stages are game abstractions, for example:
- foundational;
- general secondary;
- vocational/technical;
- university;
- postgraduate/specialist;
- continuing professional education.

KCB-WORK-050 — Education capacity, accessibility and completion time matter.

KCB-WORK-051 — Schools/universities are both service facilities and trip generators.

KCB-WORK-052 — Qualification output should lag investment, creating long-term planning.

## Institutional diversity

Education providers may include public schools, technical institutes, universities, research campuses and apprenticeship/industry programmes.

The existing atlas’s university/research districts should have distinct educational/economic roles.

KCB-WORK-060 — Old Civic and University Quarter and Administrative and University District MUST begin with meaningful education/research capacity in the Concordia scenario.

## Research workforce

Research institutions consume highly qualified labour and produce innovation capacity, specialised services, technology programme progress and prestige/attractiveness.

KCB-WORK-070 — Research cannot be reduced to a passive building bonus independent of workforce and institutional capacity.

## Workforce travel

KCB-WORK-080 — A job is effectively less attractive if commute burden is high.

KCB-WORK-081 — Severe access failure can create vacancies next to unemployment elsewhere.

KCB-WORK-082 — Remote work can reduce peak trips for eligible sectors but still requires digital/power infrastructure.

## Construction labour

KCB-WORK-090 — Construction labour is a shared city capacity.
KCB-WORK-091 — Simultaneously authorising too many major projects can lengthen schedules/increase bids.
KCB-WORK-092 — Emergency works may pre-empt ordinary construction capacity.

## Shift work

Critical and industrial facilities may operate multiple shifts.

KCB-WORK-100 — Workforce demand and transit demand can vary by time of day.

KCB-WORK-101 — Continuous services require staffing coverage, not one static employee count.

## Wages and income

KCB-WORK-110 — Income bands derive from job category, labour scarcity, productivity and policy rather than species.

KCB-WORK-111 — Wage pressure can raise operating cost and household purchasing power simultaneously.

## Workforce information views

Required:
- jobs vs workers;
- vacancies;
- unemployment type;
- qualification supply/demand;
- commute shed;
- workplace accessibility;
- shift demand;
- construction labour utilisation.

KCB-WORK-120 — Selecting a workplace shows filled jobs, vacancies, qualification mix, average commute and staffing effect on efficiency.

## Acceptance criteria

- A remote industrial district can have vacancies despite city-wide unemployment.
- New technical education improves skilled-labour supply only after time passes.
- Better transit expands a workplace’s effective labour pool.
- Automation reduces headcount but increases technical/maintenance dependence.
- Overbuilding major projects creates construction capacity pressure.
