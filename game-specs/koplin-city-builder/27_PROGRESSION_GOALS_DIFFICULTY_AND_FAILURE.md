# 27 — Progression, Goals, Difficulty and Failure

## Progression philosophy

Concordia is already a mature capital. Progression is expansion of mandate, budget, jurisdiction and modernisation options rather than rediscovery of basic urban technology.

## Progression dimensions

- stewardship reputation/mandate;
- completed strategic objectives;
- fiscal health;
- service outcomes;
- district development;
- research/modernisation capacity;
- jurisdictional expansion;
- major-project approvals.

KCB-PROG-001 — Population alone MUST NOT be the universal unlock gate.

## Strategic programme tracks

Possible tracks:
- mobility;
- housing;
- public realm;
- resilience;
- environment;
- research/innovation;
- civic/culture;
- economic development;
- infrastructure modernisation.

KCB-PROG-010 — Progress points/budget authority can be spent in branches so players shape city priorities.

KCB-PROG-011 — Eventually, long-running sandbox-like play SHOULD allow access to all non-exclusive capabilities.

## Objectives

Objective types:
- outcome target;
- project delivery;
- accessibility;
- affordability;
- service quality;
- environmental restoration;
- fiscal;
- resilience;
- heritage;
- network performance.

KCB-PROG-020 — Objectives SHOULD reward outcomes, not arbitrary placement counts.

Bad: “place five clinics.”
Better: “95% of residents within target effective primary-care access.”

## Campaign arcs

Possible default arc:
1. inherit functioning central Concordia district;
2. diagnose pressures;
3. deliver first integrated improvement;
4. expand planning jurisdiction;
5. manage multi-district growth;
6. modernise a major network;
7. coordinate metropolitan-scale programme;
8. deliver a signature Commonwealth project;
9. continue indefinitely.

## Difficulty profiles

Recommended presets:
- Creative;
- Civic Planner;
- Simulation;
- Expert.

Dimensions are separately visible:
- fiscal support;
- demand volatility;
- incident frequency;
- construction pressure;
- political friction;
- external market variation;
- citizen tolerance;
- hints/AI assistance.

KCB-PROG-030 — Difficulty does not alter fundamental simulation truth such as teleporting goods or fake capacity.

## Dynamic challenge

KCB-PROG-040 — The game MAY scale event pressure to city size, but should not rubber-band core economics merely because the player succeeds.

KCB-PROG-041 — Success creates new complexity through scale, not hidden penalties.

## Failure states

Soft failure:
- deficit;
- rising debt;
- service degradation;
- out-migration;
- stalled development;
- mandate loss.

Severe failure:
- insolvency/restructuring;
- Commonwealth/planetary intervention;
- scenario mandate revoked.

KCB-PROG-050 — Standard mode prioritises recovery over hard game-over.
KCB-PROG-051 — Intervention may impose budget controls/conditions while preserving the city.
KCB-PROG-052 — Challenge scenarios may define explicit fail conditions.

## Recovery

Tools:
- emergency grant/loan;
- asset/project deferral;
- temporary tax/fee policy;
- service mutual aid;
- phased recovery programme;
- consultancy/AI diagnostic assistance.

KCB-PROG-060 — Recovery has costs/trade-offs and is not an instant reset.

## Scoring

If scenarios score performance, use multiple dimensions:
- fiscal;
- accessibility;
- environment;
- housing;
- mobility;
- resilience;
- quality of life;
- heritage.

KCB-PROG-070 — Do not collapse the entire sandbox into one permanent score during normal play.

## Achievements

Achievements may recognise:
- elegant network solutions;
- longevity;
- zero severe outages;
- heritage;
- modal shift;
- restored waterway;
- century-old district;
- no-demolition challenge.

KCB-PROG-080 — Achievements must not require violating canon or accessibility settings.

## Acceptance criteria

- Small high-performing city can progress without population grinding.
- Expert mode increases pressure transparently.
- Fiscal failure enters recoverable restructuring in standard mode.
- Strategic tracks lead to different build sequences.
- Outcome goals can be met through multiple city designs.
